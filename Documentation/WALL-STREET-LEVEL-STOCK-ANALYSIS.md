# PART B — REPORT FORMAT CONVENTIONS

Standing instructions for how research output should be produced.

1. **Open with "What the company does"** — a short business summary before any metrics. What they sell, who buys it, how the money actually comes in.
2. **Tag every numbered point with BUY / SELL / NEUTRAL** — a signal read on that individual metric, not a recommendation on the stock.
3. **Skip the "not investment advice" disclaimer.** Keep substantive caveats about data staleness, inference vs. confirmation, and estimate reliability.
4. **Mark verified vs. unverified.** Never present an unchecked name with the same confidence as a researched one.
5. **Always check the price date.** Quote the as-of timestamp. Stale prices have caused errors.
6. **State the call explicitly** — buy, don't buy, or sell — rather than leaving it to be inferred.

## Leading indicator to watch

## Standing rules

1. **Cap total position size in dollars before adding**, not during a drawdown.
2. **Space tranches wide** — 25-30% apart on volatile names, not 10%.
3. **Keep dry powder for a capex scare.** The whole strategy pays off in a broad 25% AI drawdown — only if there's cash to deploy.
4. **Never reclassify a trade into a hold** to avoid taking a loss. The buckets stay separate.
5. **If you wouldn't buy it today, holding it is the same decision as buying it.** Cost basis is irrelevant.

# PART D — ANALYTICAL PRINCIPLES

Lessons that generalize beyond any single name.

### ATH distance is a second-order screen, not a first-order one
A great business far below its high IS a better buy than the same business near its high. But quality has to filter **first**. Sorting the watchlist purely by drawdown puts AAOI (-54%), CRDO (-47%), ARM (-43%) at the top — three of four are avoid-or-trade names. Sort by quality, *then* take the biggest drawdown.

### De-rating: good or bad depends entirely on why
Price = earnings × multiple. A de-rating is the multiple shrinking independent of earnings.
- **Good:** earnings rising while the multiple falls. AVGO's earnings roughly doubled while the stock fell 26%. You buy the same rising profit stream cheaper.
- **Bad:** the market is front-running an earnings decline. The multiple drops first, earnings follow, and cheap gets cheaper.
- **The test:** are earnings estimates being revised **up** or **down** while the price falls?

### The cyclical P/E trap
For stable businesses, low P/E = cheap. For cyclicals, E is a point on a wave, not a stable number.

| | Cycle peak | Cycle trough |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings | Very high | Very low or negative |
| P/E | **Looks cheap** (8x) | **Looks expensive** (60x+) or n/a |
| Reality | Often the top | Often the bottom |

SK Hynix: 76% operating margin at 3.9x forward. No market prices a sustainable 76% margin at 3.9x — that multiple IS a forecast. Micron went from $8.7B net income (FY22) to a $5.8B loss (FY23) while demand grew the entire time. **Memory crashes are supply events, not demand events.**

### EPS: the denominator matters as much as the numerator
EPS = net income ÷ shares outstanding. Profit can rise 30% while EPS falls 14% if the share count rises 50%. AAOI raised its share count 49.3% in one year across three offerings — every shareholder's claim on future profits shrank by a third. NVDA's share count is *down* 1.04%.

Always use **diluted** EPS. Check **GAAP** first; treat non-GAAP as the company's best case.

### PEG as the sanity check
PEG = forward P/E ÷ expected growth rate. Below 1.0, growth more than covers the multiple.

| | Forward P/E | PEG |
|---|---|---|
| NVDA | 18.1x | **0.35** |
| AVGO | ~22x | **0.50** |
| PLTR | ~92x | 1.82 |
| ARM | 101x | **3.13** |

This is how NVDA at 18x and PLTR at 92x can both be defensible while ARM at 101x isn't.

### Earnings yield ties stocks to rates
Invert P/E. A 20x P/E is a 5% earnings yield; 92x is ~1.1%. When Treasuries pay 4%, a 1% earnings yield looks bad — this is the mechanism behind every "high-multiple tech sold off on rising yields" move.

### Beat-and-raise doesn't mean the stock goes up
Sept 1-2, 2026: AVGO beat and raised, fell 5%. CRDO beat and raised, fell 19%. The difference is profitability scale and beta. When positioning is the problem rather than the P&L, results don't save you.

### Forward guidance shape matters more than the level
A maintained guide that requires H2 to run 46% above H1 (CRDO) is riskier than a raised guide that's front-loaded (AVGO). Back-loading concentrates the risk into quarters that haven't happened.
