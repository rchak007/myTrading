### Why huge targets produce LOW Benter scores

This looks wrong at first glance and is the method working. The Benter curve
**peaks around a 55–70% gap and falls away after it**: a target 4–6× the
price almost never means 4–6× upside, it means the estimate is stale,
promotional, or pricing a binary outcome. ARBE's +488% gap scores 2.0 before
the thin-coverage discount, not 10.

Coverage depth then cuts it further — 1–2 analysts is not a consensus, it is
one person's model. Only SERV has enough coverage (6) for the mean to mean
anything.

## Report 1 — ARBE (Arbe Robotics)

**4D imaging radar chipsets for automotive.**

**Goldman screener.** No P/E (loss-making). Revenue growth +157% off $0.7M —
real in percentage terms, trivial in dollars. Debt $25.9M against $41.6M cash
and a $38.4M annual burn: the debt matures into a cash position that may not
exist. No dividend. **Moat: weak.** Automotive radar is contested by
Continental, Bosch, Aptiv, Mobileye and NXP, all of whom can lose money at
this longer than Arbe can.

**IA checklist.** Space **8/10** — radar for ADAS is a real S-curve with a
genuine capex wave behind it. Moat **3/10**. The checklist fails hard at
Step 7 and Step 8:

- **Negative gross margin (−24%).** Every unit shipped destroys value. This
  is the single most important number in this report. "Revenue up, unit
  economics down" is on IA's red-flag list verbatim.
- **Serial dilution** — 64M → 109M shares, +70% in three years.
- **Massive negative FCF** — $38.4M on $1.9M of revenue.
- **1.1 years of runway.** A raise is not a risk here, it is a near-certainty,
  and it will be priced off a $0.47 share.

**Risk 10/10.** **Not investable on these frameworks.** Design wins would
change the thesis; nothing in the financials does.

## Report 2 — SERV (Serve Robotics)

**Sidewalk delivery robots.**

**Goldman screener.** Revenue +313% to $9.9M — the fastest grower here and
the only one with a real commercial deployment. **Gross margin 0%**: it
delivers at exactly cost before any overhead. Net loss $207M on $9.9M of
revenue is 21× sales burned in a year. $198M cash, $9.5M debt. **Moat: weak
to moderate** — the Uber Eats relationship is an advantage and a dependency.

**IA checklist.** Space **7/10** — last-mile autonomy is real but the
economics are unproven anywhere. Moat **4/10**. Customer concentration is
"blessing and risk" exactly as Step 6 describes.

- **0% gross margin** — the unit economics do not yet work at all.
- **Dilution +103%** in three years.
- **2.2 years of runway** at $90.8M/yr.
- **Beta 2.88** — the highest here. It will move ~3× the market.

**Risk 9/10.** The one name whose *revenue* trajectory supports the story.
Watch the **gross margin line, not the revenue line** — revenue growth at 0%
margin is activity, not progress.

## Report 3 — LWLG (Lightwave Logic)

**Electro-optic polymer modulators.**

**Goldman screener.** **$0.3M of revenue against a $677M market value —
2,331× enterprise value to sales.** There is no valuation framework in which
that is analysable; the price is entirely a claim about the future. No P/E,
no DCF worth running, no dividend. **Moat: unknown.** The polymer IP may be
genuinely defensible — that is the whole thesis — but it has not been proven
at commercial volume by anyone.

**IA checklist.** Space **9/10** — if silicon photonics modulators hit a
materials wall, polymers are the answer, and the AI datacenter capex wave is
the demand. This is a legitimate S-curve bet. Moat **unknown/5**.

- **Best balance sheet of the four**: 8.8 years of runway at a modest
  $10.9M burn, lowest dilution (+29%).
- The 99% gross margin is on $0.3M and means nothing yet.
- **Benter 5.0 NEUTRAL** — the highest of the four, and on one analyst.

**Risk 9/10, but a different shape from ARBE.** ARBE's risk is running out of
money; LWLG's is that the product never ships in volume while $677M of
expectation is already in the price. It can survive a decade being wrong.

**The number that decides it is design wins, not revenue.** Revenue will lag
a win by years; a named tier-1 customer is the signal.

## Report 4 — POET (POET Technologies)

**Optical Interposer — photonic integration.**

**The most interesting balance sheet here, and it is not obvious from the
market cap.** $877M market value, but **$796M of it is cash**. Enterprise
value is **$89M**. You are paying $89M for the operating business and the IP;
the rest you could in principle get back.

**Goldman screener.** No P/E. Revenue $1.7M, +112%. **21.8 years of runway**
at the current burn — effectively no financing risk. Debt $7.8M, trivial.
**Moat: unknown**, same as LWLG.

**IA checklist.** Space **9/10** — co-packaged optics is directly in the AI
datacenter path. Moat **5/10 unknown**.

- **Dilution +247%**, the worst here by far: 38M → 132M shares. But it bought
  $796M, which is why the EV is small. Existing holders were diluted to near
  invulnerability — a real trade-off, not a free lunch.
- **Beta 0.88** — the lowest, which is odd for a pre-revenue name and
  probably reflects the cash backing rather than any stability in the
  business.
- EV/sales 52× is high in absolute terms and the *cheapest* of the four
  relative to story.

**Risk 8/10 — the lowest here, on financing risk alone.** It cannot be forced
into a bad raise. The business risk is unchanged: $1.7M of revenue is not a
business yet.

## Ranking, and what I would actually do

On these three frameworks **together**:

1. **POET** — cannot be forced to raise, EV $89M not $877M, in the right space
2. **LWLG** — 8.8-year runway, lowest dilution, highest Benter; but $583M of
   enterprise value for $0.3M of revenue
3. **SERV** — the only real revenue ramp, but 0% gross margin and 2.2 years
4. **ARBE** — negative gross margin and 1.1 years of runway. Benter 1.7 AVOID.

**None of these passes `WALL-STREET-LEVEL-STOCK-ANALYSIS.md` as an
investment.** Every one fails the screener at the first gate (no earnings, no
margin, no cover) and three of four trip multiple items on IA's Step 8 red
flags. Benter rates one NEUTRAL and three DON'T BUY or AVOID.

That is not an argument against owning any of them. It is an argument that
they belong in the **trading bucket** of the method's own tiering, sized as
options — IA's *"beware allocations, too much size on a wrong call can be
ruin"* — not in Tier 1 or Tier 2 where the frameworks apply.

**The valuation model will decline to price ARBE, SERV and LWLG**, and should:
pre-revenue names have no honest price-to-sales anchor. A blank OracleValue
on these is the system working.
